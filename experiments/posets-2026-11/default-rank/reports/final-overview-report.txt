The strongest current choice is to keep the ranked-vector backend. The default itself has been optimized, and the separate comparison of that optimization is complete and did not show a full-solver gain: the optimized build solved one fewer case. The new lazy fractional-cascading KD tree is the closest alternative; block tries and the existing tree/trie backends did not establish a reason to replace the default in the full-solver campaign.

The first campaign ran all 1,524 SYNTCOMP26 cases for each of 15 configurations: 22,860 solver invocations. Each used the same shipping preset, 17-second strict wall cap and 8 GiB/no swap. PAR2 below is summed seconds: every unsolved case costs 34 seconds, so lower is better. This campaign predates the focused ranked-vector optimization.

The baseline returned UNREALIZABLE in 16.287 seconds; the optimized build reached an actual native timeout at 17.118 seconds and incurred the unchanged 34-second penalty. This was not a decisive answer converted into a timeout by scoring. Total PAR2 was 16.237269 seconds (0.274%) worse. Total CPU was 14,005.477 versus 14,001.094 seconds, and maximum cgroup peak was 4,047.621 versus 4,041.449 MiB. The single observed loss is real under the fixed cap. One unrepeated campaign does not establish a general regression or identify its cause. The replay and instruction reductions therefore remain useful component-level evidence, but they did not earn an end-to-end performance claim here. No rerun, retuning or selective rescoring followed the result. I would keep the ranked-vector backend; this evidence does not justify switching to a new backend or promising a synthesis speedup from the focused default patch.

The Biere/Lemire pass retained the default's two contiguous arrays and added no per-element metadata. Union and intersection reuse cached ranks; conservative exact-integral reasoning skips unnecessary equal-rank retirement tests; intersection output is deferred until containment fails. Floating and generic coordinates retain the safe fallback and tie order. Allocation/exception handling was also repaired. No additional tuning followed the measured results.

Three matched clean replay pairs show useful but uneven gains. At Acacia-compatible SIMD128, the larger round-robin predecessor improves about 4.6%, and constructing the 6,446-element full-arbiter frontier improves about 30%. Full-arbiter predecessor evidence remains unavailable: the standard cpre-6 trace timed out in source qualification, whereas the larger cpre-7 frame was structurally excluded before qualification. Smaller round-robin construction regresses about 7.8%; native SIMD512 collector predecessor regresses about 19.1%, both with mixed directions across repetitions. These percentages are medians of paired ratios, not ratios of separate medians. The report retains all 80 complete metric groups, six source-censored groups and unfavorable observations.

All 192 qualified work/heap profile calls passed. Across 990 matched phase pairs, allocation counts, requested bytes, extra tracked peak and net live-byte changes were identical. Observable result sizes and capacities matched. Rank scans and comparison attempts never increased; move assignments increased in 267 phases. The fixed four-cell hardware companion completed 24 calls with 96 usable event readings. Instructions and user cycles fell in every pair, but collector L1D read misses rose in every pair and LLC results were variable. Those pinned instrumented counters do not explain away the clean elapsed-time regressions. The default optimization reduces measured work; these captures do not establish lower heap use or a universal cache benefit.

Existing KD nodes now use 511 rather than 1,024 slots at 256 points, with capacity reuse; at 257 points the new allocation needs 1,023 slots, so reduced headroom is a tradeoff. Compressed trie nodes are 16 bytes, with bounded pending-sibling scratch. Block labels and child ranges are packed, and SIMD compares fixed coordinate blocks with safe tails. Sharing forests use generation stamps instead of per-query hash maps; stamps retain capacity over historical nodes, and dead historical roots are not automatically collected. These layout changes do not imply every workload becomes faster.

The lazy KD implementation keeps live payloads dense, uses stable IDs and live counts to skip retired subtrees, buffers new points, and rebuilds globally when configured buffer/dead-history thresholds are reached. Sampled catalogs and lower-bound bridges propagate a root search through the tree. The independent-search control uses the same geometry and leaf order. This is lazy deletion with global rebuilding, not a locally balanced dynamic KD tree with a proved amortized bound. The bulk-construction cutoff remains a heuristic.
