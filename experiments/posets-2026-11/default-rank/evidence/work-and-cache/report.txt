Default ranked-vector optimization — work and cache evidence, 9 November 2026

The default has been optimized. The completed work and hardware profiles support
keeping candidate 30e6685 for the full Acacia comparison. They demonstrate fewer
rank scans, comparisons and instructions, but no allocation or tracked-heap
reduction on these captured workloads. The separate whole-solver comparison is
still pending; this is an intermediate report.

The production change retains the existing two dense arrays. It reuses cached
ranks in union/intersection, skips unnecessary equal-rank retirement comparisons
in the conservative exact-integral domain, and delays intersection output until
containment fails. It also repairs allocation/exception handling. No additional
production tuning was introduced after the clean replay measurements.

All 192 qualified work/allocation runs passed, with no OOM or timeout. Twelve
source-censored pass records remain explicitly unprofiled. Lemire- and
Biere-guided reviews independently checked 96 matched record pairs containing
990 phase pairs. Allocation counts, requested bytes, extra tracked heap peak and
net live-byte changes were identical in every matched phase. All 822 observable
result size/capacity pairs matched; 168 ROI descriptors remain unavailable.
These repeated passes/vector controls are not 990 independent workloads.

None of the eight distinct qualified predecessor traces exercised a no-op
intersection. The positive no-op selfcheck verifies that allocation-saving path,
but it does not establish a saving on this TRAIN capture. Rank and partial-order
work never increased. Copy, meet and move-construction counts were unchanged;
move assignments increased in 267 paired phases. On the collector predecessor,
rank scans fell from 1,282 to 770, comparisons from 92,047 to 79,431, and move
assignments rose from 12,408 to 12,616. Logical changes matched across SIMD widths.

The fixed hardware companion measured four cells, three matched pairs each,
using CPU 2 and user-only grouped counters. All 24 calls and all 96 event readings
were usable, with enabled time equal to running time. No scaling, replacement
zeros, retries or extra cells were used. Percentages below are median paired
candidate/baseline ratios minus one; they are not ratios of separate medians.

| Case | User cycles | Instructions | L1D read misses |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Collector predecessor, 128-bit | -22.37% | -26.81% | +1.90% |

Cycles and instructions improved in every pair for all four cells. Collector
L1D misses worsened in every pair: the third native pair was 13.4% higher, which
the median does not show. LLC counts were small or highly variable, including a
native round-robin median regression; the raw values are retained and support
no reliable LLC benefit claim.

Earlier clean timings remain unchanged: native collector predecessor had a
19.1% median paired regression and compatible round-robin construction a 7.8%
regression, both with mixed directions across repetitions. Those regressions
did not recur in this pinned counter treatment, but they are not corrected or
causally explained. Instrumented user counters differ from unpinned elapsed
clocks; instrumentation can also affect compilation context. Three repetitions
do not establish statistical significance. Larger clean replay gains likewise
remain separate: about 4.6% for the large round-robin predecessor and 30% for
construction of the 6,446-element full-arbiter frontier at compatible128.

Tracked heap means requested C++ payload, not RSS, allocator overhead or exact
shipping object size. Operation-level caller/RHS lifetimes differ from the
normalized whole-region measurements. Whole-predecessor live delta is net
new-minus-old retained storage, not absolute frontier size. Unavailable result
descriptors are not zero retention. All observed relative cleanup deltas were
zero; that alone is not a general leak proof.

Source inspection explains a plausible move-assignment tradeoff: reserving the
vector before insertion can relocate it and then shift its suffix. Changing
reserve order could remove shifts but would need different exception-publication
handling and could increase transient heap peak. That remains an unmeasured
future option. The measured instruction/cycle gains provide no material reason
to change the qualified candidate before the solver comparison.

Baseline 789bf99 and candidate 30e6685 have the same other production headers and
replay kernel. Inputs are previously seen TRAIN data. Code commit 30e6685 passed
all current CI suites; each reported 82 passes and one intentional expected
failure. Both review workflows approved the code, and the independent profile
reviews agreed on the limitations above. These are skill-guided agents, not the
named researchers personally participating.

The accompanying files retain full paired counter data, raw PMU event values,
both ratio estimators, source/artifact bindings and independent interpretations.
They are numerical evidence, not a self-contained capture/binary archive.
publication.json records exact copied bytes and the derived report digest.
